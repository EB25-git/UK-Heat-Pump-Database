The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) explained

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) is the UK government's main grant for replacing a fossil-fuel heating system with a heat pump (or, in limited cases, a biomass boiler) in England and Wales. It's paid as an upfront discount off your installer's quote, not a cashback claim. This guide covers the current grant amounts, who qualifies, what the property and installation need to meet, how payment actually works, and how to apply.

Grant amounts

You can get one grant per property. The amount depends on what you're installing:

System Grant
Air source heat pump (ASHP / air-to-water)£7,500
Ground source heat pump (GSHP, incl. water source and shared ground loops)£7,500
Biomass boiler (off-gas-grid, rural properties only)£5,000
Air-to-air heat pump£2,500

You cannot get a grant for a hybrid system (for example a gas boiler paired with an ASHP), or to replace a low-carbon heating system that's already installed.

Temporary off-gas-grid uplift

Between 21 July 2026 and 31 March 2027, properties that are heated by oil or LPG and have no mains gas connection can get an extra £1,500 on top of the ASHP or GSHP grant — £9,000 in total. Your installer can confirm whether your property qualifies.

Who's eligible

You're eligible if you:

  • Own the property you're applying for — including a business premises, a second home, or a property you rent out to tenants.
  • Are replacing a fossil fuel heating system: oil, mains gas, electric, or LPG.
  • Have a property in England or Wales. Scotland has its own Home Energy Scotland loan/grant; Northern Ireland's Boiler Replacement Scheme is now closed.
  • Get the system commissioned and installed within 120 days of your installer applying for the grant, or it stops being eligible.

Already had funding to make the property more energy efficient (loft or wall insulation, for example)? That doesn't affect your BUS eligibility. Self-build properties are also eligible for a heat pump (though not a biomass boiler) if you or the original owner built it, or paid a builder to build it, and it has never been owned by a business — you may need to show your installer proof, such as the title deeds, if you built it yourself.

Property & installation requirements

  • MCS-certified installer and product. Both the installer and the specific heat pump model must be on the MCS register — this is now a statutory requirement, not just guidance. Check a product on this database is MCS-certified before assuming a BUS grant applies to it.
  • No EPC required. As of 28 April 2026, you no longer need a valid Energy Performance Certificate to apply, and the old rule requiring outstanding loft/cavity-wall insulation recommendations to be resolved first has been scrapped. Where a property has no EPC, the installer submits a recent utility bill and photos of the existing heating system instead.
  • Maximum system capacity: 45kWth for a single system, 70kWth where a heat pump is installed alongside one or more additional heat pumps, and 300kWth for shared ground loops.
  • Biomass boilers additionally need the property to be off the gas grid and in a rural location, and the boiler must have an emissions certificate confirming low emissions. Biomass isn't available for self-build properties.

What doesn't qualify

  • New-build properties still being built by a developer. If you move into a finished new build with a fossil-fuel boiler, you may still qualify for a heat pump grant later.
  • Social housing.
  • A property that's already received government funding or support for a heat pump or biomass boiler.

How payment actually works

The grant isn't something you claim back after paying in full — it's built into the price you're quoted from the start.

  • Your installer deducts the grant upfront from your installation quote and invoice. You only pay the balance.
  • Once you've agreed a quote, the installer applies for the grant on your behalf through Ofgem's online system — you don't apply yourself.
  • Ofgem may contact you to confirm the installer is genuinely acting on your behalf, including by phone or a visit to your property; MCS may separately carry out its own checks.
  • The scheme is not means-tested — it's available regardless of income — and the grant does not need to be repaid.

How to apply

  1. Contact MCS-certified installers for quotes.
  2. Confirm you're eligible — your installer will check this with you.
  3. Agree a quote. The grant amount is shown as a deduction on the quotation and final invoice, and your installer applies for it on the Ofgem website once you've agreed to go ahead.

Scheme duration & funding

BUS launched in April 2022 as a 3-year scheme and has since been extended twice — following the June 2025 spending review, it's now funded through to 31 March 2030, with £400 million budgeted for 2026–27 alone, the largest annual allocation since launch. The rules have changed more than once since launch, most recently in a substantial overhaul on 28 April 2026 (removing the EPC requirement, adding air-to-air heat pumps, and putting MCS certification on a statutory footing). Always check the official GOV.UK guidance for the current position before budgeting for a project.
