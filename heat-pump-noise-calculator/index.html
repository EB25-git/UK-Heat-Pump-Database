Heat Pump Noise Calculator

Check whether an air source heat pump meets the 37 dB(A) permitted development noise limit at your neighbours’ windows, using the official MCS 020 a) calculation for England. Pick a heat pump from 2,167 models with published sound power data, describe where it will go, and add every neighbouring window or door that could be affected.

Free to use, no subscription. This follows MCS 020 a) Issue 1.1 exactly, but it is a planning aid: your installer must still complete and keep the MCS 020 a) calculation for the installation. Other permitted development conditions also apply.

Neighbours

Assessment positions

An assessment position is 1 m outside the centre of any window or door to a habitable room (bedroom, living room, dining room, study) of a neighbouring home, on every side and every floor. The nearest window is not always the worst one – a further window with a clear line of sight can be louder. Bathrooms, hallways, landings and utility rooms do not count. In a block of flats, other flats in the same block count.

How the calculation works

MCS 020 a) predicts the sound pressure level at each assessment position from the heat pump’s sound power, how much hard surface surrounds it, the distance and any solid barrier:

Lp = LW + 10·log10( Q ÷ 4πr² ) − AB

Line of sight is judged from the top edge of the heat pump: no view if the barrier completely hides the assessment position, partial view if it becomes visible when you move up to 0.25 m, full view if it is visible. A barrier must extend at least 1 m beyond each side of the unit.

With two heat pumps the levels at a position are added logarithmically: Lp,total = 10·log10(10Lp1/10 + 10Lp2/10). Two equal units are 3 dB louder than one. The result is rounded to 0.1 dB and must be 37.0 dB(A) or lower at every assessment position.

Worked example (MCS 020 a) Appendix A): a 60 dB(A) heat pump on the ground against one wall (Q4), 8.0 m from a first-floor bedroom window, behind a brick wall with a partial view (5 dB): 60 + 10·log(4 ÷ (4·π·8²)) − 5 = 32.0 dB(A) – compliant.

Where this applies

  • England: since 29 May 2025 an air source heat pump installed under permitted development must meet MCS 020 a) (37 dB LAeq,5min). This applies whether or not the installer is MCS certified. The same changes removed the 1 m boundary rule, raised the size limit to 1.5 m³ and allow two heat pumps on a detached house.
  • Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland: installations still follow the earlier MCS 020 standard and their own permitted development rules, which this calculator does not cover.
  • Meeting the noise limit does not by itself make an installation permitted development. Listed buildings, Article 4 areas and some other sites need planning permission. See our planning permission guide.

Source: MCS 020 a) Issue 1.1, Air Source Heat Pump Sound Calculation (MCS, 14 April 2025) and MCS: New Permitted Development rules in England. Sound power figures come from manufacturers’ published data in this database – always confirm against the current product fiche.
